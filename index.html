Thesis

🎓 Bachelor Thesis

Interpretable Video Game Recommendation

My thesis investigates whether sparse autoencoders — an interpretability technique proven on large language models — can decompose a game recommender's item embeddings into human-interpretable, steerable concepts, and whether that depends on the SAE architecture or on the underlying embedding space. I built the full offline research pipeline in Python (PyTorch, scikit-learn, pandas, NumPy) on a large public Steam dataset under a chronologically realistic split, benchmarking 7 collaborative-filtering models (ALS, Modified ALS, ELSA, EASE, kNN) to select two structurally different embedding backbones, then designed 4 SAE architectures under a hard Top-K sparsity constraint across a full hyperparameter grid and ran offline steering experiments across 11,733 users. Key finding: interpretability depends far more on the backbone's embedding geometry than on the SAE architecture. The discovered concepts power a self-contained web app where users can steer their own recommendations by tag.
